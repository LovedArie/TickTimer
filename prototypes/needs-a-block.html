Plan vs. actual · 30-minute slots
1The gap this fills. Task blocks already exist — you can click a free slot and pick a task, or type a task name into a block's label. What the app never does is notice that an urgent task has no time set aside for it anywhere. You can create "Lab 4 — due Friday", mark it Urgent, and get zero signal that Friday is coming and nothing is planned. That silence is the whole feature.

2Why the glance panel. It's already the "state of my day" surface, and it's beside the agenda — so the fix (a free slot) is in view at the same moment as the problem. A banner across the top would be louder, but loud is not obviously right here. Flip the knob and see which you'd tolerate every single morning.
3"Find time" is a suggestion, never an action. Clicking it highlights the free runs that fit — it does not silently book anything. The app's whole thesis is plan-vs-actual honesty; an app that fills your calendar on your behalf is writing a plan you never made. The model can propose later (that's the secretary arc), but the human still presses the button.
4"Not today" is load-bearing, not a courtesy. This app is built for anxiety-driven procrastination. A list you cannot quiet becomes a list you learn to ignore — and once you're ignoring it, every other signal in the app loses credibility too. Dismissal is what keeps the feature honest: it can be right to not schedule something today, and the app should be able to hear that.

Open questions — your call

The ones I couldn't settle alone. Write on top of these.
  1. Does a block on a future day count as scheduled? Prototype says yes — a task with time booked Thursday doesn't nag on Tuesday. But then a task due Wednesday with a block on Thursday looks fine and isn't. Worth catching, or over-thinking it?
  2. How long does "Not today" last? Until midnight is the obvious answer. But a task you dismiss every morning for a week is telling you something — should the app eventually stop asking rather than keep asking?
  3. Should overdue-and-unscheduled read differently from urgent-and-unscheduled? Prototype shows both in one list with different chips. They may deserve real separation — overdue is a fact, urgent is an opinion you set.
  4. Does this belong on the week view too? Untouched here. "Nothing planned all week for a Friday deadline" is arguably the more useful warning, and the week view is where you'd act on it.
  5. Is "Needs a block" the right name? Alternatives considered: "Unscheduled", "No time set aside", "Waiting for time". The current one names the fix rather than the failure — a deliberate tone choice, but yours to overrule.
