2Why gating earns its keep. The panel is a fixed, narrow column. Adding a card would push the category bars below the fold on a laptop — so the card would compete with the very numbers it sits next to. Taking the panel over costs nothing, because unscheduled urgent work is the more useful thing to know first. When the list is empty the panel is exactly what it always was.
3Two settings, OR'd — not three presets. Your default ("urgent + due ≤3d") is really a priority set plus a due window, and they're independent. Modelling it that way means someone who wants "only ever urgent" sets the window to Off, and someone who wants "everything this week" sets priorities to none and the window to 7 — without the app shipping a preset for each combination. Open Settings and watch the live preview count change.
4Where this rule lives in the real code. needsBlock(task) is a pure function of the task plus the two settings — no widget state. In the build it belongs in the domain beside Task.h, with the settings read through prefs::, not inside GlancePanel. Otherwise the panel, and later the week view, each grow their own subtly different answer — the exact drift eventLabel() was extracted to stop.

Still open — your call

Reduced from five; two got answered by your notes.
  1. Does a block on a future day count as scheduled? Prototype says yes ("Read chapter 7" is blocked Thursday and stays quiet). But a task due Wednesday with a block on Thursday looks handled and isn't. Catch it, or over-thinking?
  2. Does the gate re-arm the next day? Prototype assumes yes — one look per day, reset at midnight. The alternative is re-arming whenever a new task qualifies, which is quieter but means a deadline can creep up without a fresh prompt.
  3. How long does "Not today" last? Until midnight here. A task dismissed seven mornings running is telling you something — should the app stop asking, or say something else?
  4. Does this belong on the week view too? Still untouched. "Nothing planned all week for a Friday deadline" may be the more useful warning, and the week view has room to act.
  5. Is "Needs a block" the right name? It names the fix rather than the failure — deliberate, but yours to overrule. ("Unscheduled" / "No time set aside" / "Waiting for time")

Settings — Needs a block

Which open tasks should the glance panel flag as having no time set aside?
Always flag these priorities
A task at one of these priorities is flagged whatever its due date — including tasks with no due date at all.
Also flag anything due within
Catches an ordinary task whose deadline is closing in. Overdue tasks are always flagged.
Hold today's numbers until I've looked
The glance panel shows this list first. Your focus/break totals and life-area bars appear once you've reviewed it — or immediately, if nothing needs a block.
